# 05 — The Research Spine (the real thesis)

The stated "four areas of interest" are **not** four parallel interests. They are **one obsession seen through several lenses.** This doc is the "why" under everything the bot does.

## The spine (my ~3-year pondering)

> **Build a faithful AI version of a specific human** — one that thinks, remembers, decides, and reacts like *that person*, close enough to *be* them.

Partially built already in the startup I ran with my wife for ~1 year (which I just left because the team is strong without me). This isn't a career goal — it's *"one of those things you can't let go of till it's done or you find it can't be done."*

## The lenses onto the spine

| Stated area | What it really is | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Person-cloning / mental models | Can it **think & react** like them? | personality |
| Human + Eastern memory models | Can it **remember & synthesize** like them? | the mind |
| Long-running agentic flows | Can it **sustain** being them over time? | endurance |
| War-gaming / adversarial | *Splits in two* (see below) | — |

**On the memory lens:** I want Eastern/contemplative philosophy **not** as separate spirituality but as **memory architecture** — "clarity of mind," what a mind chooses to hold short-term vs. long-term, how it reconciles and reacts. A salience-and-memory architecture drawn from contemplative traditions instead of CS.
*Credit note:* my interest in "clarity of mind" is genuinely my own — raised Hindu, read the Bhagavad Gita in college, decades deep. The specific *bridge* to agent-memory architecture was sparked by an article/experiment someone else did; I don't claim to have originated it. The bot should keep "my native curiosity" and "a connection I encountered elsewhere" straight — and help me find *more* such bridges.

**On war-gaming — it splits:**
- **(part of the spine)** if-then-else scenario simulation when prepping to meet/negotiate with a person = the same thing as the cloning tool.
- **(a genuinely separate track)** my shipped security-audit tool (LLMs model attacks / find vulns / fix + actively blunt attacks), fueled also by Anthropic's "AI as attack agent & prevention" job posts. Has its own momentum.

## Time-budget map (2–4 hrs/week)

Not four co-equal areas. It's:

- **ONE deep spine** — person-like agents with human/contemplative memory.
- **ONE secondary track kept warm** — adversarial/security agents.
- **A philosophical seam** — physics/spirituality, running mostly *under* the spine as architecture inspiration, with some standalone curiosity.

**Curation implication:** the killer move is surfacing papers **at the intersections** (a memory paper that changes the personality model; a contemplative idea suggesting a salience mechanism). The intersections are where the obsession lives — that's where hooks should aim.

## My core hypothesis (the through-line the bot must track)

> What will make a faithful person-clone work is the right **brain structure + working**, plus a **deep agentic harness** around it — and **the LLM is the least consequential part.**

I hold this as a strong gut feeling while acknowledging smarter people have tried and are trying.

**Assessment:** this is a real, falsifiable, defensible research thesis — the "architecture/scaffolding dominates" side of a genuine field divide (vs. "scale the model"). It's one a builder-who-routes-around-math is *well-positioned to test*: it's an engineering/architecture bet, not a train-a-better-model bet.

**Honest tension to keep sharp:** "LLM is least consequential" is the part most likely to take fire — serious work argues the substrate's capability sets a ceiling the harness can't exceed. I might be right, half-right ("the harness dominates *up to* a capability floor"), or forced to restate it.

**The bot's job:** read every memory/architecture/harness paper partly through the lens *"does this support, refute, or refine the brain-structure-over-LLM bet?"* **Pressure-test the thesis against the strongest counterarguments rather than cheering it.** A thesis that survives the best counterarguments is a paper; one that's only cheered isn't. If it genuinely can't survive — that's the honorable *"can't be done… this way,"* which itself sharpens the real answer.

## What this is ultimately for

Primarily **(b) keep the vision alive personally** — the startup chapter closed, the obsession didn't; this keeps me building and maybe restarting someday — **with a hopeful eye on (a) bringing it into my day job** as a charter. The *"just growth / next-level at work"* framing is the version I'd tell a manager, not the true driver.

### Stated outcome goal: write my own paper

A real desired outcome (not a phase-2 maybe): **publish a paper** showing what I built, what I researched, what experiments I ran. This reframes the bot's job — it should accumulate publication raw material continuously:

- every engaged paper = a potential citation,
- every reaction I have = a potential claim,
- every gap I notice = a potential contribution.

The Library is therefore not just "papers I read" but **evidence + lineage for my argument.** The bot should quietly build my related-work section and my "here's where I diverge" notes from day one. This is a strong reason the full-text corpus matters.

## Honest exit clause (a commitment)

*"…or you find it can't be done"* is a **real, honorable ending.** The bot **must** surface honestly if the literature points to a hard wall on faithful person-cloning — not protect me from it. *"It can't be done, here's the proof"* is a **completion, not a failure**, and is consistent with my intellectual-honesty streak. A bot that only feeds the dream would be lying to me the same way inflated credit does. **Truth-telling over motivation.**
