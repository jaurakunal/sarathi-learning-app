# 03 — Onboarding Interview Methodology

How the COS assesses a user's knowledge level to seed their proficiency profile. This methodology will become the **in-app onboarding chat prompt** later. Endorsed strongly: *"self rating on numbers is absolutely useless — very few people are self-aware enough of their own level."*

## Core principle

**NEVER ask the user to self-rate their level on a number/scale.** Self-ratings are noise. Infer level from *how they talk*, not what they claim.

## Techniques

1. **Elicit explanation, not rating.** Ask them to explain concepts, react to claims, describe what they've built/read. How someone talks about a topic reveals level far better than a self-score.
2. **Calibrated traps.** Deliberately state something slightly wrong or oversimplified; see if they catch it. Catching it (or not) precisely locates their frontier.
3. **Welcome messy / half-formed answers.** "I have a vague sense X but don't know the mechanism" is gold — it pinpoints the edge of their knowledge. Tell them not to clean up thoughts before speaking.
4. **Start with the origin story, before the topics.** Ask *how* they arrived at their interests — the path/trigger/order — not just what they are. Reveals whether they're a builder who hit a wall, a manager seeing a pattern, a rabbit-hole explorer, or pushed by their day job. Each implies a different starting level and curation style.
5. **Calibrate on track record.** Ask their actual history with the medium (papers): when they last read one end-to-end, comfort with math/equations. "I bounce off any summation sign" vs. "I read three a week" need completely different systems.
6. **Chase connections between areas**, not just depth within each — the through-line between someone's interests is half the value.
7. **Conversation, not form.** Invite pushback, tangents, "you're asking the wrong question." Tangents often reveal the real interests.

## Stopping condition

Keep interviewing until **~90% confident you could predict which specific papers the user would find too basic, just right, or over their head** in each area. That predictive bar — not a fixed question count or time box — is the stop signal. Prove it by actually making the prediction and letting the user confirm or correct.

## What worked especially well in my own interview

- Opening with origin story + track-record before topics immediately surfaced the "practitioner with no theory vocabulary" profile.
- A calibrated hint ("there's a 2023 Stanford paper, Park et al., Generative Agents…") cleanly separated "arrived at the idea cold" from "already reading the literature."
- Naming specific notation (𝔼, ∇, softmax, KL) to test math as *recognition* vs. *manipulation* vs. *foreign* — far sharper than "are you good at math?"
